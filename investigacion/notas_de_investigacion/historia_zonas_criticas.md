# Historical El Niño flood impacts and recurrent critical zones on Peru's northern coast (Tumbes, Piura, Lambayeque, La Libertad, Áncash)

Research date: 2026-11-10. Note for the report writer: impact figures from INDECI/COEN change a lot with the reporting cut-off date ("fecha de corte") and with scope (the whole rainy season vs. a single event). Each figure below keeps its cut-off date where known, so do not add up figures from different cut-offs.

## 1. Quantitative impacts per event (1982–83, 1997–98, 2017, 2023): rainfall, peak flows, deaths, affected people, homes, economic losses

### Takeaway
1982–83 and 1997–98 were the costliest events relative to GDP: 11.6% of 1983 GDP and 6.2% of 1998 GDP (some sources give about 4.5%). The 2017 El Niño Costero cost about US$3.0 bn (1.6% of GDP) and killed about 143–169 people nationwide. In Piura, the river peaked at about 3,400–3,468 m³/s in 2017. The 1997–98 peak was about 3,900–4,400 m³/s, depending on the source. The channel through the city carries only about 1,900 m³/s. The 2023 season (Ciclón Yaku plus El Niño costero) was smaller in Piura, with a peak of about 1,700 m³/s. It was severe in Trujillo's quebradas, and nationally it killed about 85 people.

### Cited Findings
**Economic losses (all events)**
- 1982–83: losses of US$3,283 million, equal to 11.6% of 1983 GDP. Affected population was 1,267,730, of whom 831,915 were in the north and 435,815 in the south. 587,120 people were left without housing. — [Andina, 15-jun-2023](https://andina.pe/agencia/noticia-como-impacto-nino-peru-los-eventos-19821983-19971998-y-2017-943812.aspx)
- 1982–83: the BCRP puts the impact on the productive sectors at 5.3% of GDP. This is a different basis from the 11.6% total. — [BCRP, Reporte de Inflación jul-2014, recuadro 1](https://www.bcrp.gob.pe/docs/Publicaciones/Reporte-Inflacion/2014/julio/ri-julio-2014-recuadro-1.pdf)
- 1997–98: damages of US$3,500 million, equal to 6.2% of 1998 GDP. Of this, US$1,612 M (46%) was direct and US$1,888 M (54%) indirect. More than 42,000 homes were destroyed. The most affected areas were Piura, Tumbes, Ica, Chiclayo, Trujillo and Chimbote. — [Andina 2023](https://andina.pe/agencia/noticia-como-impacto-nino-peru-los-eventos-19821983-19971998-y-2017-943812.aspx)
- 1997–98: CAF material gives Peru's losses as about 4.5% of GDP. The BCRP, citing CAF, gives US$1,625 M (2.9% of GDP) for the productive sectors only. — [CAF, Las lecciones de El Niño – Perú (dipecholac)](https://dipecholac.net/docs/files/996-publicacion-caf-peru-nino-97-98.pdf); [BCRP 2014](https://www.bcrp.gob.pe/docs/Publicaciones/Reporte-Inflacion/2014/julio/ri-julio-2014-recuadro-1.pdf). The sources conflict (4.5% vs. 6.2%), so report both.
- 2017 El Niño Costero: about US$3,000 million (1.6% of GDP), per the BCRP. 162 deaths and almost 1.5 million affected, per "authorities at the time". — [Andina 2023](https://andina.pe/agencia/noticia-como-impacto-nino-peru-los-eventos-19821983-19971998-y-2017-943812.aspx)

**Peak flows**
- Piura river at Los Ejidos dam, 27-mar-2017: the measured peak was 3,468 m³/s. The early warning system had forecast 2,900 m³/s on 26-Mar. Recalculating from the recorded levels gives 4,023 m³/s, 16% above the reported value. The dam rehabilitation alternatives were sized for 4,700 m³/s. — [Reyes et al., LACCEI 2020](https://laccei.org/LACCEI2020-VirtualEdition/full_papers/FP275.pdf)
- Piura river at Puente Sánchez Cerro: more than 3,400 m³/s in 2017 and about 1,700 m³/s in March 2023. — [Mongabay 2023](https://es.mongabay.com/2023/03/desbordes-inundaciones-por-lluvias-en-piura-peru/). An official quoted elsewhere gave a 2017 peak of 3,100 m³/s, and the critical flood threshold at Sánchez Cerro is 1,900 m³/s. — [El Comercio](https://elcomercio.pe/peru/piura-ano-tragedia-situacion-actual-nino-costero-noticia-507536-noticia)
- The urban channel of the Piura river can carry about 1,900 m³/s. Peaks were almost 3,900 m³/s in 1998 and about 3,400 m³/s in 2017 (J. Reyes, UDEP, Jul-2026). — [La República, 29-jul-2026](https://larepublica.pe/sociedad/2026/07/28/ingeniero-advierte-que-el-rio-piura-solo-puede-conducir-la-mitad-del-caudal-registrado-durante-el-nino-de-1998-1242444); [UDEP](https://www.udep.edu.pe/estamos-en-los-medios/2026/07/29/el-nino-alertan-que-rio-piura-solo-podra-soportar-la-mitad-del-caudal-registrado-en-1998/). Another source gives 4,400 m³/s for 1998 ([Semana](https://www.semana.com/peru-lluvias-del-nino-costero-sigue-haciendo-estragos/37429/)), and América TV says "cerca de 4,000 m³/s" for both 1983 and 1998 ([América TV](https://www.americatv.com.pe/noticias/actualidad/piura-proyecto-infraestructura-podria-evitado-inundaciones-n271344)). The 1998 value conflicts across sources (3,900 vs. 4,400 m³/s).
- Ranking of Piura river mean annual flow at Sánchez Cerro over the last 100 years: 1998 > 1983 > 2017 > 1925 (IGP). — [IGP Boletín Técnico, 2017, comparing 1925 and 2017](https://repositorio.igp.gob.pe/bitstreams/2baeeea5-aea6-429b-84ef-7736fb0cdbbb/download)

**Rainfall**
- At the Universidad de Piura station, rainfall totalled 410 mm by mid-March 2017, against 160 mm in all of 2016. — [UDEP Hoy](https://www.udep.edu.pe/hoy/2017/lluvias-atipicas-con-unas-consecuencias-muy-tipicas/)
- The Miraflores station in Piura recorded 93.3 mm in 24 h in a later event, above the 87.2 mm daily value of March 2017. — [Correo](https://diariocorreo.pe/peru/fenomeno-el-nino-piura-soporto-la-peor-lluvia-desde-1998-noticia/?outputType=amp)
- In 2023, rain that normally falls over 30 days in March fell in 3–4 days in Piura. Canchaque had 75 landslides in 24 h. — [Mongabay 2023](https://es.mongabay.com/2023/03/desbordes-inundaciones-por-lluvias-en-piura-peru/)
- SENAMHI's table of 24-h rainfall records for Jan–Mar, comparing 2017 with 1983 and 1998, is a useful primary source, but I could not parse its values reliably. — [SENAMHI informe006-dma](https://www.senamhi.gob.pe/usr/dcl/informe006-dma.pdf)

### Inferences
- In Piura city, flooding happens when flow at Sánchez Cerro goes above about 1,900 m³/s, the channel capacity. That threshold was exceeded by about 1.8–2x in 1983 and 1998 and about 1.8x in 2017. It was not exceeded in 2023 (about 1,700 m³/s), yet the city still flooded through pluvial ponding in "cuencas ciegas". The model therefore needs two target mechanisms: fluvial overflow and pluvial/drainage ponding.
- In 2017, damage per department scaled with urban exposure (Lambayeque and Piura hold 68% of collapsed properties), not only with rainfall.

### Gaps
- I did not find a verified, sourced series of seasonal rainfall totals (mm) for Piura-Miraflores, Chulucanas or Morropón in 1983, 1998 and 2017. My prior (unverified) recollection is that Piura recorded about 2,000+ mm in 1983 and 1998 against a normal of about 50–100 mm. Check SENAMHI or the IGP before using it.
- I found no sourced peak flows for the Chira, Tumbes, La Leche, Motupe, Chancay-Lambayeque, Moche, Virú, Santa or Lacramarca rivers in any event. ANA/SENAMHI hydrological reports would be needed, for example [SENAMHI Informe Técnico Hidro N°003-2017](https://cdn.www.gob.pe/uploads/document/file/3274125/Informes%20T%C3%A9cnicos%20hidro%20N%C2%B0%2000003-2017.pdf), which I did not parse.
- I found no 2023 per-department death or damnificado breakdown for Tumbes, Lambayeque, La Libertad or Áncash, and no BCRP/MEF estimate of 2023 losses as % of GDP.
- I found no 1982–83 death toll by department.

## 2. Which districts and cities are hit again and again (recurrence)

### Takeaway
The sources consistently show these places flooding in two or more events:
- Lower and middle Piura: Piura, Castilla, Veintiséis de Octubre, Catacaos, Cura Mori, Bajo Piura/Sechura, Tambogrande and Chulucanas.
- Metropolitan Trujillo: El Porvenir, Florencia de Mora, Trujillo, Víctor Larco, La Esperanza, Huanchaco, El Milagro and Laredo, through the San Ildefonso, San Carlos and El León quebradas.
- Lambayeque: Chiclayo, and the La Leche plain at Jayanca, Pacora and Íllimo.
- Áncash: Huarmey, Chimbote, Casma and Nepeña.
- Tumbes city.

I found no official ranked district list from INDECI or CENEPRED. The ranking below is my synthesis.

### Cited Findings
- 1997–98 named epicentres: Piura, Tumbes, Chiclayo, Trujillo and Chimbote. — [Andina 2023](https://andina.pe/agencia/noticia-como-impacto-nino-peru-los-eventos-19821983-19971998-y-2017-943812.aspx)
- 2017 (27-Mar) Piura river overflow: urbanizaciones, asentamientos and centros poblados in the districts of Piura, Castilla and Catacaos were flooded. — [El Comercio](https://elcomercio.pe/peru/piura-ano-tragedia-situacion-actual-nino-costero-noticia-507536-noticia); [Mongabay 2017](https://es.mongabay.com/2017/03/dia-despues-las-consecuencias-los-desbordes-e-inundaciones-del-rio-piura/)

The critical river reach runs from Puente Grau to Chato Chico (Bajo Piura). — [Correo/ANIN](https://diariocorreo.pe/edicion/piura/anin-presentara-avances-de-megaproyecto-ante-inundaciones-en-piura-noticia/); [Gestión/Contraloría, 14-mar-2025](https://gestion.pe/peru/contraloria-advierte-retrasos-en-obras-del-rio-piura-incrementa-riesgo-de-desbordes-noticia/)

Laredo was also affected. — [INGEMMET Repo-1937-31](https://app.ingemmet.gob.pe/biblioteca/pdf/Repo-1937-31.pdf); [Correo 2026](https://diariocorreo.pe/edicion/la-libertad/a-nueve-anos-del-desastre-trujillo-sigue-expuesto-la-libertad-peru-noticia/). - Áncash 2017: Huarmey was the worst hit; Chimbote, Casma, Nepeña, Culebras, Lacramarca and Santa were also affected. — [INGEMMET via SIGRID 3677](https://sigrid.cenepred.gob.pe/sigridv3/documento/3677/descargar)

Proposed recurrence tiers for use as labels. These are a synthesis and need validation against SINPAD/SIGRID emergency counts per district.

## 3. Physical and social drivers of vulnerability

### Takeaway
The recurring drivers are:
- A Piura river channel that carries only about half of extreme El Niño peaks and is silting up.
- Flat, drainage-less cities with "cuencas ciegas" (closed depressions) and no storm sewer system.
- Urban growth onto dry quebrada beds, as in Trujillo's 19 quebradas.
- Unplanned settlements and missing riverbank defenses in the middle and lower basins.

### Cited Findings
- The Piura channel carries about 1,900 m³/s, about half of the 1998 peak. Urgent actions proposed are descolmatación, riverbank reinforcement, pumping stations and a relief outlet at Chutuque. The storm-drainage works in central Piura remain unfinished (Jul-2026). — [La República 2026](https://larepublica.pe/sociedad/2026/07/28/ingeniero-advierte-que-el-rio-piura-solo-puede-conducir-la-mitad-del-caudal-registrado-durante-el-nino-de-1998-1242444)
- Contraloría (Orientación de Oficio N°001-2025-OCI/7176-SOO) found a silted river bed with shrubs, trees and sediment islets between Av. Andrés Avelino Cáceres and Av. Bolognesi, a reduced hydraulic section, and no periodic desilting. — [Gestión, 14-mar-2025](https://gestion.pe/peru/contraloria-advierte-retrasos-en-obras-del-rio-piura-incrementa-riesgo-de-desbordes-noticia/)
- In Piura, Castilla and Catacaos, "cuencas ciegas" filled up to 1 m deep, sewers overflowed and there is no storm drainage. Tambogrande has no riverbank defenses. — [Mongabay 2023](https://es.mongabay.com/2023/03/desbordes-inundaciones-por-lluvias-en-piura-peru/)
- INGEMMET attributes Trujillo's high number of damnificados in 2017 to unplanned urban growth over the quebradas. — [INGEMMET Repo-1937-31](https://app.ingemmet.gob.pe/biblioteca/pdf/Repo-1937-31.pdf)
- More than 400 people living by the El León quebrada had still not been relocated in Feb-2021. — [Exitosa](https://www.exitosanoticias.pe/trujillo/trujillo-mas-400-damnificados-fenomeno-nino-aun-son-reubicados-n46722/amp)
- The CENEPRED El Niño scenario (2026) builds exposure from observed impacts of 1982–83, 1997–98, 2015–16, 2017 and 2023, and from population, housing, schools, health facilities, roads and farmland data (INEI, Minsa, Minedu, Midagri, MTC). — [La República, Jul-2026](https://larepublica.pe/sociedad/2026/07/02/lluvias-por-el-nino-ponen-en-riesgo-muy-alto-a-mas-de-93-millones-de-peruanos-lima-tiene-la-mayor-exposicion-199940)

### Inferences
Candidate predictors for the 2027 model:
- Height above nearest drainage / floodplain membership.
- Distance to a quebrada thalweg.
- Share of the urban area built after 2000 inside active channels.
- Storm-drain coverage, which is near zero in Piura.
- Downstream position relative to channel-capacity bottlenecks (Puente Grau–Chato Chico).
- Informal housing and poverty rate (INEI).
- Past emergency counts (SINPAD).

### Gaps
- I found no quantitative poverty, informality or vulnerability index per district in the sources consulted. CENEPRED's district-level vulnerability layers (SIGRID) would need direct download.
- I did not access the academic papers named in the brief (French et al.; Rodríguez-Morata et al.; Takahashi & Martínez; Sanabria et al.) in full. Only the IGP bulletin referencing Takahashi & Martínez (1925 vs. 2017) was retrieved, and it was not parsed in detail.

## 4. CENEPRED risk scenarios and prioritized districts (recent seasons)

### Takeaway
CENEPRED's mid-2026 El Niño costero scenario puts 209 districts in 22 departments at "muy alto" flood risk, with 7.95 M people exposed. Piura is second only to Lima, with 1.36 M people exposed. La Libertad follows with 0.87 M and Lambayeque with 0.55 M. Piura also leads in exposure to mass movements (huaicos), with 0.28 M people.

### Cited Findings
- CENEPRED El Niño costero scenario, reported in July 2026. Flood risk at "muy alto": 209 districts in 22 departments; 7,951,322 people; 2,315,202 homes; 4,076 health facilities; 14,728 schools; 5,877 km of roads; 1,360,206 ha of farmland. Mass-movement risk at "muy alto": 185 districts in 16 departments; 1,383,000 people; 308,534 homes. — [La República, 2/8-jul-2026](https://larepublica.pe/sociedad/2026/07/02/lluvias-por-el-nino-ponen-en-riesgo-muy-alto-a-mas-de-93-millones-de-peruanos-lima-tiene-la-mayor-exposicion-199940)

## 5. Status of reconstruction and prevention works (ARCC → ANIN) as of 2026

### Takeaway
As of 2026, the "soluciones integrales" for Piura (and, by extension, Chira and Tumbes) are still at the study or viability stage. Nine years after 2017, structural flood risk in Piura city has not been materially reduced. The Plan Integral del río Piura is not expected before 2029. Drainage is unfinished, the river bed is silted, and in Trujillo only partial quebrada works exist.

### Cited Findings
- The ARCC budget was more than S/ 23 bn (about US$6 bn) for about 8,000 interventions in 13 regions. The Plan Maestro del Río Piura is estimated at about S/ 4 bn and had no technical files as of 2023. — [Mongabay 2023](https://es.mongabay.com/2023/03/desbordes-inundaciones-por-lluvias-en-piura-peru/)

### Inferences
- For a 2027 model, treat Piura (Piura, Castilla, 26 de Octubre, Catacaos, Bajo Piura) as having unchanged or higher structural hazard compared with 2017: the channel capacity is still about 1,900 m³/s, it is silting, and no integral defenses or drainage are built. Trujillo's San Ildefonso basin may have partially reduced hazard from the retention ponds, if they were completed in 2026; this is not confirmed. The Chicama and Virú defenses are not reduced.
